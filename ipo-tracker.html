What happened to the IPOs you remember?

Every one of these arrived with a queue, a grey market premium and somebody on television explaining why this one was different. Here is the boring part nobody runs afterwards: what they are worth now, measured from the day you could first buy them.

Why most of these numbers are reported wrong

A bonus issue is not a crash

When a company issues bonus shares or splits its stock, the price per share drops mechanically and your holding multiplies to match. You have lost nothing. But the raw price history does not know that, so a stock that did nothing wrong can look like it collapsed, and a quiet winner can look ordinary. Two of the sixteen below are badly misreported if you skip this check.

It cuts both ways, which is the part worth remembering. The same oversight that invents a collapse at one company hides a fivefold understatement at another. Every figure on this page has been checked against the exchange's own record of splits, bonuses and dividends.

What this does and does not say

Read this before you draw a conclusion

It does not say IPOs are bad

Two of these turned one rupee into several. It says the odds of picking which one are not what the queue outside the application window implies.

These are not all the IPOs

It is a named list of listings people remember, not a complete cohort. Treat it as sixteen specific companies, never as "IPOs returned X".

Dividends are not counted

These are price returns only. The companies that paid dividends did better than shown. Most of the ones that fell paid nothing.

We are not telling you what to buy

This is arithmetic on published prices, not a view on any company. Picking one for you is advice, and that takes a registration we do not hold.

How this was worked out

Method

The two prices

Entry is the closing price on the company's first day of trading on the NSE, taken from the exchange's daily record. Exit is the closing price on .

The adjustment

Every company was checked against the exchange's corporate action record. Where a split or bonus happened, the listing price is restated into today's share terms before any return is calculated. Dividends are recorded but not added back.

The returns

The headline figure is the total change from entry to exit, not annual. The second figure is that same return expressed as a yearly rate, so a five year holding and a two year holding can be compared honestly.
