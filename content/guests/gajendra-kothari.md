Gajendra Kothari is Managing Director and CEO of Etica Wealth, a mutual fund distribution and financial planning firm he has built over the better part of a decade. The name is the Spanish and Portuguese word for ethics, and the firm states its philosophy as ethics before everything.

## What they care about

Etica works on goal-based plans rather than product sales, and deliberately serves middle net worth families rather than chasing the ultra wealthy: business owners, salaried professionals, retirees, single women and NRIs across more than twenty five countries. Alongside mutual funds the firm advises on term and health cover and on estate planning, on the view that a plan with a hole in its protection is not a plan.

He is a CFA charterholder and has spent two decades in the industry, and he sells mutual funds only. On the show he made the case for that narrowness rather than apologising for it. Direct stocks demand that you understand a business well enough to own it for twenty years, which almost nobody has the bandwidth to do across more than a handful of names. Bonds hand you a capped upside and an uncapped downside, which he calls the reverse of the game equity offers.

His argument for patience is not theoretical. He started a ₹10,000 monthly SIP sixteen years ago, never once topped it up so the track record would stay clean, and has put in roughly ₹19.5 lakh. It is now approaching a crore. He today runs a SIP of around ₹50 lakh a month, funded by the business rather than by the market, and is blunt that the business is the machine and the SIP is only where its output goes.

The part worth sitting with is what he thinks an adviser is for. Borrowing a framework from Nick Murray, he puts a fifth of the job in quantifying goals and building the plan, and the other four fifths in making sure you stick to it through every cycle. His words: my role is not to choose the best fund, not to time the market, not to make you rich quickly, but to stand as a shield against the noise coming your way. Most of the time the answer he gives a client is no.

Which is why, when people ask him for the best fund, he says the question is wrong. Hand an ordinary player Sachin Tendulkar's bat and no century follows. The bat was never the point.
