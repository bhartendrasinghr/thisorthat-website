The SEBI rulebook, in plain language

Every fund fits a box. SEBI just redrew the boxes.

42 boxes. One yardstick each. And two numbers nobody shows you together: how much it grows or how hard it can fall.

In February 2026, SEBI rewrote the rulebook that decides what every mutual fund is allowed to be. We went through the whole thing so you do not have to. For each box below you get four things: the rule a fund in that box must follow, the yardstick it is measured against, the long run growth that yardstick has historically delivered, and the worst 12 months it has ever put investors through. Read the last two together, always.

So, return chaser or drawdown survivor?

Here is our side of the fork: pick your category by the worst 12 months column, not the growth column. Growth is what the market gives everyone who stays. The drawdown decides whether you stay. If a 60% fall would make you sell, that category's long run 18% was never yours to begin with.

This is not a no, it is a not-like-this. If a box scares you, that is useful information, not a failing. Move down a band and stay invested, which beats picking the brave box and leaving at the bottom.

*Long run growth is the approximate 10 year CAGR of the category's benchmark or category average as of July 2026. Worst 12 months is that benchmark's worst historical one year return, mostly the 2008-09 crash. Figures are indicative and rounded, and they will drift: treat them as shape, not gospel. Riskometer labels are category norms, and every scheme publishes its own each month. Category rules are summarised, so check the SEBI circular and the scheme's own documents before you act on any of it.
