Category Compass · a model, not a tip

Which equity fund category, right now?

Nobody can tell you which category will win next year. What the numbers can tell you is which ones look cheap or expensive against their own past, which are rising or falling, and which are calm or jumpy. Put together, that tells you which way to lean.

Live from NSE. Data as of , updated with every site build.

What we actually did
1. Took ten years of real index data

Weekly closing price, price to book and price to earnings for seven equity categories, from the NSE's own archive. Roughly 550 weeks per category, back to 2016 where it exists.

2. Asked three questions of each

Is it cheap against its own past? Is it rising? Is it calm? Each answer becomes a number out of 100, by ranking today against every week that came before.

3. Mixed them into one score

Cheap counts 40%, rising 35%, calm 25%. Those are a starting point, not a truth. Change them below and every number on this page moves.

4. Then checked whether it ever worked

We replayed the score every week since 2018 using only what was knowable at the time. It helped over the following year. Over three years it made no difference at all. Both results are below, unedited.

What the score is not: it is not a forecast, and it is not about any particular fund. It ranks seven market indices against their own history. A high score means a category looks cheap, or strong, or calm compared with how it has usually looked, and nothing more than that.

    Open book

    How the score is built

    Valuation: is it cheap?

    Compares today's price to book and price to earnings with every week since 2016. Lower than usual scores higher. It also checks how much more mid and small caps cost than large caps. When that gap is wider than usual, the smaller categories lose points and large caps gain them.

    Momentum: is it rising?

    Half the points come from one question: is the index above its average price of the last 40 weeks, roughly 200 trading days? The other half come from how its 6 and 12 month returns compare with the whole market, the Nifty 500.

    Risk: is it calm?

    How much the index has swung up and down over the last 6 months, against its own past. Plus India VIX, the market's fear gauge, which is at today. Calmer than usual scores higher.

    We score indices, not funds. An index has clean daily price, P/E and P/B data going back years. A fund's NAV mixes in one manager's skill and costs. Your fund can do better or worse than its category's index.

    Before you act

    How to use this without hurting yourself

    Start from your mix, not from this page

    Your split between large, mid and small caps should come from your goals and how long you can wait. This page only nudges that split a little.

    Steer new money first

    The cheapest way to tilt is to point fresh SIPs or lump sums towards the favoured category. Selling to switch costs you tax and sometimes an exit load, which eats most of the small edge shown above.

    Check it every few months

    The ranking moves slowly. Looking at it every week will tempt you to act on noise. Once a quarter is plenty.
